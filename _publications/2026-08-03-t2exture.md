Passive long-wave infrared (LWIR) imaging remains effective under adverse illumination, but it often lacks the fine texture needed for detailed perception. Existing thermal-texture imaging approaches commonly depend on spectral sensing or registered auxiliary modalities, which can increase data throughput or introduce cross-modal degradation.

This work introduces **T²exture**, a sparsely perturbed thermal-to-texture imaging framework that reconstructs temporally dense thermal-texture sequences from densely sampled passive frames and a few actively perturbed keyframes. It defines thermal texture as the residual between a source-on observation and its corresponding source-off passive state, isolating a source-induced response that reveals localized material- and geometry-dependent texture.

T²exture first estimates the unobserved source-off passive state at each active instant to form reliable differential-texture anchors. It then combines these sparse anchors with passive structural context to reconstruct a dense texture sequence. On the simulated benchmark, the method adds 0.20M parameters to AMT-L while improving PSNR by 6.66 dB; experiments on simulated and real acquisitions show improved texture recovery and structural preservation over representative VFI baselines.
