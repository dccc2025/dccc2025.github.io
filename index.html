Selected Works
Agents OVOK: A Recursive Self-Improving Agentic Framework for Video-Based Articulated Robot State Estimation
Cheng Dai*†, Renkai Luo*, Guanfeng Tang, Siming Li, and Hao Zhao
CVPR 2027 under review
* Equal contribution; † Project Lead.

We propose OVOK (One Video to Kinetics), a recursive self-improving agentic framework for recovering articulated robot state from video. The evolved workflow achieves state-of-the-art results on DREAM-V and RoboKeyGen-V.

Agents GaIR-Agent: Gaussian Inverse Rendering via Closed-Loop Reasoning and Physical Refinement
Siming Li*, Renkai Luo*, Cheng Dai, Guanfeng Tang, and Hao Zhao
CVPR 2027 under review
* Equal contribution.

We propose GaIR-Agent, an agentic framework for Gaussian inverse rendering that recovers editable materials and illumination. On FIPT-Synthetic and NeuSky Synthetic it reaches state-of-the-art relighting and cuts material-target generation time by 85.6% indoors and 90.2% outdoors versus a non-agentic pipeline.

Paper

4D Recon IGGLoop: Instance-Grounded Gaussian Looped Transformer for Feed-Forward 4D Driving Scene Reconstruction
Guanfeng Tang*, Renkai Luo*, Cheng Dai, Siming Li, and Hao Zhao
CVPR 2027 under review
* Equal contribution.

We propose IGGLoop, an instance-grounded framework for feed-forward 4D driving scene reconstruction that unifies instance segmentation, tracking, Gaussian reconstruction, and motion estimation. On Waymo it gains 2.57% PSNR over the state-of-the-art pose-free baseline, with 6.4% of its parameters and a 3.45× speedup, and the recovered Gaussian assets support instance insertion, removal, and repositioning.

Paper

Agents FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research
Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, ..., Cheng Dai, ..., Gerbrand Ceder, Hartmut Haeffner
Nature under submit

FrontierPhysics is an end-to-end benchmark of 56 physicist-authored research tasks across nine theoretical, computational, and experimental domains. Agents must review the literature, carry out the research, and write a manuscript, scored for correctness and publishable quality. Across 22 models in 26 configurations, none passed more than 46 of 168 rollouts; the main gaps were rigorous reasoning and expert intuition, not physics knowledge or execution.
